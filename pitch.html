Moment 02 · Channel-first capture

Moment 03 · Assisted structure

The engine drafts—but does not pretend certainty.

The system groups the incident, transcribes the Lao voice note and separates problem, cause, resolution, verification and warnings.

Platform response

Highlight low-confidence transcript segments, missing evidence and potentially sensitive values.

Recovery path

The expert corrects meaning in context; the original source and transformation history remain traceable.

Human authority

AI prepares a candidate. It cannot publish, grant access or claim the resolution is safe.

Investor meaning

Automation lowers structuring burden while visible uncertainty creates a defensible trust boundary.

Moment 04 · Governed approval

An accountable reviewer decides the exact submitted version.

The reviewer compares material changes, source coverage, sensitivity and policy checks before approving, returning or rejecting.

Decision object

One immutable version, content hash, source package and independent reviewer.

Control boundary

Only an authorized role can decide; restricted evidence never leaks through the approval view.

Evidence created

A version-bound decision receipt records who decided, why and against which evidence.

Investor meaning

Reusable knowledge earns authority through workflow evidence—not through an opaque AI answer.

Moment 05 · Grounded reuse

The next developer asks a real operational question.

The answer presents supported statements, citations, partial evidence and the exact source version relevant to the current incident.

Question

Platform response

Return evidence-backed steps, show limits and stop before an unsupported production change.

Human authority

The answer informs judgement; it does not execute a command or grant production authority.

Each governed capture can compound through faster retrieval, safer reuse and better onboarding.

Moment 06 · Continuity control

The incident becomes role-owned capability.

The manager can see the owner, review date, freshness, successor practice and any remaining single-expert exposure.

Next action

01
The diagnostic

Twelve causes across four connected layers.

Knowledge loss is not one documentation problem. It is a system in which individual behaviour, organisational design, process timing and invisible economics reinforce one another.

Layer 1 · Person

Critical knowledge is difficult—and sometimes unsafe—to share.

  • Tacit judgement is hard to write down.
  • Experts skip context that feels obvious to them.
  • Withholding can be rational under weak incentives or low trust.
  • Expert identity may be tied to being uniquely needed.
Layer 2 · Organisation

The structure concentrates knowledge in individuals.

  • The busiest people hold the most critical context.
  • Knowledge belongs to people instead of roles.
  • Leadership lacks an inventory of what could be lost.
  • Single-mentor onboarding creates one fragile link.
Layer 3 · Process

Capture begins after the risk is already urgent.

  • Exit is used as the trigger, when time and trust are lowest.
  • No role-level continuity event starts preparation early.
Layer 4 · Economics

The loss remains invisible until it becomes an incident.

  • Rework, delay and recovery cost are rarely measured together.
  • Hiring is mistaken for replacing accumulated judgement.
The reinforcing cycle

Overload becomes loss.
Loss creates more overload.

  1. 01Busy experts defer capture.
  2. 02Knowledge attaches to one person.
  3. 03A departure or absence removes context.
  4. 04Survivors absorb rework and incident load.
  5. 05The next capture becomes even less likely.
Why this matters to an investor

The problem is recurring, measurable and poorly served by static documentation.

Recurring trigger

Roles change, systems evolve and incidents repeat. Continuity is an operating need, not a one-time migration.

Workflow wedge

The platform starts where knowledge already moves—in selected work conversations—rather than asking experts to maintain another diary.

Compounding value

Every reviewed capture can improve retrieval, onboarding, handover and risk visibility within the customer boundary.

Plain-language guide

Five terms that make the product easier to judge.

Open only the concepts you need. The rest of the pitch uses these meanings consistently.

Governed knowledge +

Knowledge with a named owner, authorized sources, review state, access boundary, version and next review date—not merely text stored in a workspace.

Grounded answer +

An answer whose material statements link to exact approved sources and explicitly show uncertainty, limitations or missing evidence.

Continuity case +

A controlled workspace that coordinates knowledge coverage, participants, gaps, successor practice and evidence for one role or service.

Knowledge gate +

An accountable decision point that checks required evidence before a knowledge case can close; it does not complete HR or grant production access.

Channel-first capture +

The expert explicitly selects useful context from an existing work channel instead of leaving work to maintain a separate knowledge diary.

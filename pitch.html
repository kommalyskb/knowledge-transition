01
The diagnostic

Twelve causes across four connected layers.

Knowledge loss is not one documentation problem. It is a system in which individual behaviour, organisational design, process timing and invisible economics reinforce one another.

Layer 1 · Person

Critical knowledge is difficult—and sometimes unsafe—to share.

  • Tacit judgement is hard to write down.
  • Experts skip context that feels obvious to them.
  • Withholding can be rational under weak incentives or low trust.
  • Expert identity may be tied to being uniquely needed.
Layer 2 · Organisation

The structure concentrates knowledge in individuals.

  • The busiest people hold the most critical context.
  • Knowledge belongs to people instead of roles.
  • Leadership lacks an inventory of what could be lost.
  • Single-mentor onboarding creates one fragile link.
Layer 3 · Process

Capture begins after the risk is already urgent.

  • Exit is used as the trigger, when time and trust are lowest.
  • No role-level continuity event starts preparation early.
Layer 4 · Economics

The loss remains invisible until it becomes an incident.

  • Rework, delay and recovery cost are rarely measured together.
  • Hiring is mistaken for replacing accumulated judgement.
The reinforcing cycle

Overload becomes loss.
Loss creates more overload.

  1. 01Busy experts defer capture.
  2. 02Knowledge attaches to one person.
  3. 03A departure or absence removes context.
  4. 04Survivors absorb rework and incident load.
  5. 05The next capture becomes even less likely.
Why this matters to an investor

The problem is recurring, measurable and poorly served by static documentation.

Recurring trigger

Roles change, systems evolve and incidents repeat. Continuity is an operating need, not a one-time migration.

Workflow wedge

The platform starts where knowledge already moves—in selected work conversations—rather than asking experts to maintain another diary.

Compounding value

Every reviewed capture can improve retrieval, onboarding, handover and risk visibility within the customer boundary.

The intervention portfolio

Five pillars break the cycle at multiple points.

No single exit checklist can fix a system-wide problem. The solution combines low-friction capture, role ownership, successor coverage, incentives and visible governance.

A · Capture engine

Make capture continuous and easy.

Guided conversations, micro-capture and event-triggered after-action review turn operational moments into reviewable drafts.

B · Structure

C · Incentives

D · Timing

Act before a departure.

A continuous default and role-level continuity triggers prepare coverage while time and trust still exist.

03
The adoption system

Reduce friction without removing control.

Sixteen recurring burdens explain why knowledge programmes quietly decay. The Knowledge Transition Standard turns the lesson into four practices and two guards that a team can adopt progressively.

Friction budget≤15 min

A design target for routine contributor capture—not a proven outcome. Real pilots must measure time, completion, quality, accessibility and exceptions.

  • No blank-page authoring
  • Approval rather than duplicate writing
  • Voice, text and assisted paths
  • Maintenance routed to named owners
P1

Name your crown jewels

Identify the knowledge whose loss would interrupt the role or service.

P2

Capture in 15 minutes

Use a guided, bounded path instead of a blank document.

P3

Assign a living owner

Every governed item has an accountable person and review date.

P4

Pair before you lose

Successors demonstrate capability before continuity is claimed.

G1

Zero-friction guard

Remove avoidable burden while preserving lawful and high-risk controls.

G2

Departure verification

Intensify the knowledge case without delaying employment or access obligations.

The product system

From work conversation to governed continuity.

The product is not another document editor. It is a controlled loop that captures selected operational context, routes uncertainty to people and returns useful knowledge to the workflow.

01

Capture in-channel

An expert explicitly forwards, mentions or selects useful work context in an authorised channel.

02

Structure a candidate

The engine groups source context, transcribes where needed and drafts problem, cause, resolution and verification.

03

Govern before release

Sensitive data, confidence, provenance and access boundaries remain visible to the accountable reviewer.

04

Apply and transfer

Approved knowledge supports grounded answers, onboarding and handover with source citations.

05

Monitor continuity

Owners see stale knowledge, concentrated expertise and transitions that need attention.

Product experience

See how the continuity system works.

Explore six connected workspaces from the real 64-screen interactive design baseline. Each view shows a different part of the controlled journey—from assigned work to executive continuity decisions.

Interactive design prototypeFictional demonstration data · English UI mode shown · not production software or customer traction
SC-003 · Role-aware home

Start with the next authorized action—not a generic dashboard.

A developer sees why each task appears, what is overdue or blocked and the exact action their role may take next.

  • Assignment and authority are visible
  • Blocked work explains its dependency
  • No employee performance score is created
SC-006 · Capture & ingest

Turn selected work conversations into reviewable candidates.

The website is the control room—not the knowledge diary. Experts remain in their channel while reviewers inspect source scope, security and confidence.

  • WhatsApp-first capture path
  • Searchable intake and processing states
  • Human review required before release
SC-013 · Governed approval

Decide the submitted version—and nothing else.

An accountable reviewer compares material changes, source coverage and policy checks against one immutable review object.

  • Independent reviewer boundary
  • Version and content hash remain visible
  • Decision evidence stays traceable
SC-022 · Evidence-guided answer

Judge the answer before you act.

Every statement can expose what supports it, what remains uncertain and which exact source version applies to the incident.

  • Statement-level citations
  • Partial support remains explicit
  • A generated answer grants no production authority
SC-016 · Continuity case

Coordinate transfer without claiming false completion.

The workspace identifies the next material blocker, accountable owner, evidence needed and the safe route toward a knowledge gate.

  • Coverage and gaps remain separate
  • Privileged-access boundaries stay intact
  • Knowledge completion does not imply HR or production handover
SC-030 · Leadership decisions

Turn continuity exposure into accountable direction.

Leaders begin with the decision that needs attention, inspect bounded evidence and record a rationale without turning knowledge data into employee ranking.

  • Decision priority before dashboard totals
  • Evidence readiness and limitations visible
  • Organizational outcomes remain supporting context

06
The business hypothesis

Land on a visible continuity risk. Expand with governed knowledge.

The commercial model is deliberately a hypothesis until customer discovery and a real pilot validate buyer urgency, willingness to pay, adoption burden and measurable value.

Initial buyer

Technical leadership

IT managers and engineering leaders responsible for service continuity, onboarding and concentrated operational expertise.

Initial users

Experts, owners and successors

Contributors capture in existing channels; owners review; successors retrieve and practise; managers monitor coverage.

Entry workflow

Incident-to-knowledge

Start with recurring incidents and troubleshooting where context loss, rework and time-to-recovery can be measured.

Revenue hypothesis

Governed B2B SaaS tiers

Subscription tiers can follow team scale, governed usage and deployment needs. Pricing is not claimed until willingness-to-pay evidence exists.

Expansion path

Team → function → enterprise

Expand from technical continuity into onboarding, cross-functional handover, governance packs and controlled on-premise needs.

Regional wedge

Lao-first, multilingual

Serve Lao operational teams with English and Thai foundations, then validate which adjacent Southeast Asian markets share the workflow.
